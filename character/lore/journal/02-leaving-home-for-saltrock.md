# Journal Entry 02: Leaving Home for Saltrock

*Written at seventeen, on the night I left Alzenhiem and reached Saltrock.*

Today was a beautiful day.

That feels strange to write on a day of goodbye, but it is true. The sky over Alzenhiem was clean. The lane smelled like warm dust and cut grass. Even the light on our roof looked soft, as if the hamlet itself was trying to send me off kindly.

I left home after saying goodbye to my family one last time.

Astiale did not want to let go. She never does. She pressed her face into my new shirt and held on like a small star refusing to set. I knelt so she could reach me properly. I told her I would come back. I did not tell her how large the world is, or how easily roads can steal years. She is eight. She should keep believing that brothers return when they say they will.

It is always hard leaving Alzenhiem. That place is still my center. Barracks can teach my hands. Prophecy can chase my name. None of that moves the quiet truth: when I close my eyes and look for home, I find dirt paths, low roofs, and the doorway where Dolkin’s shadow used to fall at dusk.

My parents understood that I had been appointed Hero. Understanding did not make them love the leaving. Fulfein’s eyes were clear and wet at once. She did not argue with Yua’s charge. She argued with the shape of a mother’s fear: that the world asks for her son and may not give him back whole.

Dolkin was quieter. He always is when the weight is worst. He still carries lead in him from the day he sent me to the yard at eight. He never expected that choice to grow into this: a title older than rumor, a road with no promise of return dates. I know he hates the choice he made, even if he will never say it aloud. Fathers like him do not confess guilt in speeches. They confess it in gifts heavy enough to mean stay alive.

Before I left, he placed a new sword in my hands. One he made. The balance was clean. The steel felt like his work always feels: honest, unfussy, meant to last. He called it a goodbye. I heard the real sentence underneath.

I love you, son. Please come home.

He also gave me his first set of smithing tools. The ones that taught his own hands. He looked at me the way he looks at a blade he trusts, and said:

“When you’re on your own, you are the master of your steel. Treat it well and it will never betray you.”

I will keep those words next to the tools. A Hero’s title can be stripped by failure. A smith’s care cannot. If I maintain what I carry, I carry a piece of Dolkin into every fight.

Fulfein handed me ten gold pieces, a cooking pot, a set of new clothing, and a shovel.

The gold is practical. The pot is love disguised as sense: eat warm when you can. The clothing was folded the way only she folds things, seams true, no wasted cloth. I put the new clothes on before I left, out of respect and appreciation. It felt like wearing her blessing in fabric form.

The shovel made her smile first. Fulfein is practical enough to pack gold and a pot, and mother enough to pack dignity for the road’s least dignified chore. When a Hero has to go number two, the woods still expect him to dig like everybody else. She called it a different kind of duty, and Kalhien almost choked trying not to laugh.

I laughed too. Then the shovel sat a little heavier in my hands than a joke should.

Because roads take people. Not everyone makes it through. Sometimes a shovel is for privacy behind a tree. Sometimes it is for giving the dead a place that is not a ditch and a forgetting. I do not think Fulfein said that part aloud. She did not have to. Mothers who send children into danger pack hope in funny shapes, and still pack for the truth underneath.

I will try to need it only for the funny reason.

Kalhien stood a little apart, jaw set, trying to look older than fifteen. He clasped my forearm hard enough to mean both pride and anger at being left behind again. I told him to watch the house. He told me not to become so legendary that I forget how to come home ordinary. Fair.

I said my final goodbyes, held Astiale one more time, bowed my head to my parents, and walked the lane out of Alzenhiem with red and black on me and Alzenhiem still under my ribs.

I am in Saltrock now.

Saltrock is a traveling town, a place where many come and go. Carts, traders, sellswords, messengers, people between one life and the next. I know it well. During training years I passed through enough times that its noise feels familiar instead of threatening.

It is also the first place I ever saw an item that truly used magic.

In the general building sits a staff, floating above its place as if the air itself had agreed to hold it. Beneath it, stones bear names. The staff protects the town from any monster or creature whose name is written there. I was younger the first time I saw it, and I remember stopping so suddenly someone nearly walked into my back. Magic, real magic, not rumor and fading stories. A tool with will enough to guard a whole restless town. Even now, knowing Yua, knowing what is dying in the world, that staff still steadies me. Proof that wonder is not finished yet.

I am lodging at the tavern for the night. They always serve the best bread… if you only have nostalgia attached to it. Otherwise it is actually quite bland. Soft enough, warm enough, missing the soul Fulfein’s kitchen puts into crust. But do not ever say that out loud in Saltrock. Some truths are safer as private jokes between a journal and its owner.

Tomorrow I hope to find a good place to travel. A direction. A purpose with boots on. Tonight I sit with a new sword, my father’s first tools, my mother’s gold and pot and shovel, clean clothes that still smell faintly of home, and a town full of people who do not know that the boy eating bland bread was just named Hero.

Yua has not shown herself much today. Maybe she is giving me the goodbye without interruption. Maybe she knows some thresholds a mortal should cross with only family in his hands.

If Alzenhiem is my center, then today I walked away from the center on purpose.

I will try to make that mean something worth the lead in my father’s chest, and worth the way Astiale’s fingers left marks on my sleeve.

*Dezco Knoleburn*
*Saltrock, night of departure*
