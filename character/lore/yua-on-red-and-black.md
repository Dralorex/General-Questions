# Why You Love Red and Black

*Lore note for later. Dezco does not know this yet.*
*When the time comes, Yua tells him. Not as a lesson from books, but as something she has always seen in him.*

## How it may be spoken

Yua does not explain it all at once. She waits until Dezco asks why the colors have always felt like home, or until a moment of doubt makes the question matter. Then she answers quietly, as if the truth had been sitting beside him since childhood.

She might begin like this:

“You think you chose red and black because they please your eye. You did not choose them. They chose the shape of your blood long before you learned their names.”

## Black

Black is the Dwarven half of him.

Deep beneath the mountains, the Dwarves did not fear the dark. They loved it. Their cities were carved into caves where sunlight never reached, where stone held its breath for ages, where a torch was a guest and not a ruler. To them, black was not emptiness alone. It was shelter. It was home. It was the quiet that let craft and patience grow without the glare of the surface world.

Yua ties that love to Dezco without softening it.

Black is also the unknown. The empty void. The place where sight fails and faith, or fear, must take its place. When evil rises and begins to swallow the light, the first color left behind is black. Not always as comfort. Often as warning. The caves that cradled a people can also be the mouth of something that wants the world sealed shut.

So black in him is both inheritance and omen:

1. The Dwarven love of deep places, of dwelling where the world is stone and shadow.
2. The void that waits beyond knowing.
3. The darkness that takes over when light is driven back.

He carries all three without knowing he carries them. That is why black feels familiar. Not because it is gentle, but because part of him was made to walk inside it and not be lost.

## Red

Red is the Elven half of him.

Among the Elves, white was purity of the clean and untouched kind: snow, moonlit cloth, the bright hush of sacred groves. Red stood against that white as something just as holy, though born from wound. They found blood pure. Not because pain was good, but because life made itself known in crimson. When something ill happened, red appeared. It marked the moment the world was hurt, and also the moment the world insisted it still lived.

To the Elves, red was not merely violence. It was sacred contrast. White without red could become a lie of perfection. Red without white could become cruelty. Together they told the truth: that holiness can live in what spills when harm is done, and that life answers injury with color too honest to ignore.

Yua names this in him as well.

Red only arrives after something has gone wrong. A cut. A battle. A cost. Yet the Elves blessed that arrival. They saw in blood a holy thing that refused to pretend the world was unscarred. So Dezco’s love of red is not a hunger for harm. It is an old Elven knowing that life declares itself most clearly when the clean light has been broken, and something warmer, truer, takes its place for a while.

## Why they match

Black and red belong together because each answers the other.

Black is the dark cave, the void, the evil pressing over the light. Red is the blood that appears when that darkness, or any ill, has already touched the living. One is the deep place and the swallowing night. The other is the proof that life still answers from inside the wound.

Neither color alone is the whole truth.

Black without red becomes a void that never pays its cost, a darkness that forgets what it has taken. Red without black becomes only the scream of injury with nowhere to dwell, no depth to hold the meaning of what was lost. Together they form a balance:

1. Shelter and sacrifice.
2. Unknown depth and living proof.
3. The dark that can cradle or consume, and the blood that holy tradition refuses to call unclean.
4. Dwarven stone night and Elven sacred crimson, joined in one body.

That is why Dezco loves them without being able to explain why. His Dwarven blood remembers the cave. His Elven blood remembers the holiness of blood against white. His soul, standing between fading magic and rising dark, keeps choosing the two colors that already speak his purpose: to walk into black without becoming it, and to honor red as life’s answer when something ill has come.

## What Yua wants him to understand

When she finally tells him, she is not flattering him. She is warning him and blessing him in the same breath.

She might end like this:

“Black is where your people of stone learned to live. It is also the void that tries to eat the light. Red is the holy blood your people of leaf and star refused to call impure, the color that comes only after harm, and still means life. You love them because you are both, and because the world needs someone who can hold both without tipping into either alone. Balance is not soft, Dezco. Balance is red and black standing together when the white of easy innocence is gone.”

Until that day, he only knows that he loves the colors.

He does not yet know they have always been telling him who he is.
