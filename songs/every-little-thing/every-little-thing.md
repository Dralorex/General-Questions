# Every Little Thing

*A general happy song about noticing the small good things — no capo, key of D, warm and bright.*

Not about anyone in particular; not about a relationship. A porch-light,
kettle-on-the-stove, drive-with-windows-down kind of song, meant to land
wherever your own version of "small good things" lives.

---

## The bones

- **Capo:** none
- **Key:** D major
- **Tempo:** ~♩ = 92 (medium-bright, unhurried, front-porch pace)
- **Feel:** warm acoustic folk-pop — think Jack Johnson / Sheryl Crow / Passenger territory

## Chord shapes  *(E A D G B e, low to high)*

```
D     : x x 0 2 3 2
A     : x 0 2 2 2 0
Em    : 0 2 2 0 0 0
G     : 3 2 0 0 0 3    (add high open for Gadd/G6: 3 2 0 0 3 3)
Bm7   : x 2 0 2 0 2    (easy voicing — no barre needed)
Cadd9 : x 3 2 0 3 3    (used only in the outro tag)
```

All beginner-friendly. If `Bm7 = x20202` gives you trouble, `x20232` is a
fuller alternative that still avoids the barre.

## Strumming

- Primary pattern: **`D — D-U — U-D-U`** per bar.
- Laid-back alternative: **`D — D — U-D-U`**.
- Let the open strings ring — this song's whole sound is the top two
  open strings (B and e) singing through every chord.

## Progressions

| Section     | Chords                                                       |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Verse       | D — A — Em — G                                               |
| Pre-Chorus  | G — A — Bm7 — A                                              |
| Chorus      | G — D — A — Bm7   *(twice; the second one lands on D)*       |
| Bridge      | Bm7 — G — D — A                                              |
| Outro       | G — D — A — D — G — Cadd9                                    |

## Full song

### Verse 1
```
[D] The kettle whistles, [A] morning finds the floor.
[Em] The neighbor's dog stretch-[G]es out toward the door.
[D] There's nothing much happ'n-[A]ing at all today —
[Em] just the sun through the win-[G]dow doing its slow, gold way.
```

### Verse 2
```
[D] A friend calls up just [A] to say hello.
[Em] A stranger lets you [G] cross before they go.
[D] A song comes on the radio [A] you used to love —
[Em] and the whole day feels [G] like something to be proud of.
```

### Pre-Chorus
```
[G] It's not the mountain [A] tops, and it's [Bm7] not the highs —
it's the ordinary [A] miracles you catch out of the corner of your eyes.
```

```
[G] It's every little [D] thing,
[A] every little [Bm7] thing —
[G] the ordinary [D] blessings
[A] that you don't notice till they [D] sing.
[G] Every cup of coffee, [D] every open window sky,
[A] every "how you doing?" [Bm7] from a stranger passing by.
[G] It's every [A] little [D] thing.
```

### Verse 3
```
[D] The porch light's on when [A] you get back home.
[Em] Somebody left a note that [G] said you weren't alone.
[D] The dog runs over like [A] you've been gone a year —
[Em] and it hits you sudden, [G] how good it is to be here.
```

### Bridge
```
[Bm7] The days will get heavy, [G] the news will get loud,
[D] but the good is still right here [A] if you slow yourself down.
[Bm7] A porch light in the evening, [G] someone saving you a seat,

```

### Final Chorus  *(open up — strum a little harder, let the high strings ring)*
```
[G] It's every little [D] thing,
[A] every little [Bm7] thing —
[G] the tiny holy [D] moments
[A] that you're lucky just to [D] sing.
[G] Every kitchen conversation, [D] every drive with windows down,
[A] every "thank you" that meant [Bm7] more than they knew somehow.
[G] It's every [A] little [D] thing.
```

### Outro  *(back to gentle strumming, ritardando on the last line)*
```
[G] Every little [D] thing... [A] every little [D] thing...
[G] every little [Cadd9] thing.
```

*End on the Cadd9 and let it ring — that added D against the C sounds like
sunlight through a window. Warm, hopeful, not quite finished.*

## Why this works without being cheesy

- **D–A–Em–G in the verse** = I–V–ii–IV. That ii chord (Em) is what keeps
  this from sounding like a generic beach commercial. It's a warm,
  slightly-thoughtful chord in the middle of an otherwise bright
  progression — the tiny bit of gravity that makes the sunshine feel earned.
- **The chorus starts on G, not D.** That means the chorus feels like the
  sky *opening up* rather than resolving. It doesn't come home until the
  very last chord of each phrase.
- **Concrete lyrics.** Kettle, dog, porch light, coffee, someone saving you
  a seat. Concrete images are what let a "happy" song land instead of drift
  into vagueness — everyone has their own version of each of these details.
